# 1. Residuals vs Fitted Values
What to look for: Random scatter around the horizontal line at y=0

Good: Points randomly scattered with no pattern → linear regression assumption is valid
Bad: Points form a pattern (U-shape, funnel, curve) → suggests the relationship isn't linear or variance isn't constant
For your report: "The residuals appear randomly distributed around zero, indicating [good/concerning] adherence to the linearity assumption..."

# 2. Q-Q Plot (Normality Check)
What to look for: Points following the diagonal line

Good: Points closely follow the red diagonal line → residuals are normally distributed
Bad: Points deviate from the line, especially at the tails → residuals are not normally distributed
For your report: "The Q-Q plot shows residuals [closely following/deviating from] the normal distribution line. This suggests residuals are [approximately/not] normally distributed..."

# 3. Predicted vs Actual
What to look for: Points close to the red dashed diagonal line

Good: Points cluster tightly along the diagonal → predictions are accurate
Bad: Points scatter widely away from the line → predictions are poor
For your report: "Predicted values align [well/poorly] with actual values, with an R² of X.XXXX, meaning the model explains X% of the variance in goal scoring differences..."

# 4. Residual Histogram
What to look for: Bell-shaped curve centered at 0

Good: Roughly symmetric, bell-shaped distribution → residuals are normally distributed
Bad: Skewed, bimodal, or flat distribution → normality assumption violated
For your report: "Residuals show a [roughly normal/concerning] distribution, with most errors concentrated around zero..."
